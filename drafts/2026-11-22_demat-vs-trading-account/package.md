## 1. Topic and unique angle
The difference between a demat account, a trading account and a bank account. Angle: "Demat alone is not enough." Beginners hear all three words together and assume they are one thing.

## 2. Target audience and objective
Hindi-speaking beginners about to open an account. Objective: a clean mental model (demat = locker, trading = order route, bank = money), a habit of checking the broker's SEBI registration, plus saves and shares.

## 3. Three hook options
1. **"सिर्फ़ डीमैट अकाउंट काफ़ी नहीं है। तो शेयर ख़रीदने के लिए और क्या चाहिए?"** / "A demat account alone is not enough. What else do you need to buy shares?"
2. "डीमैट और ट्रेडिंग अकाउंट एक ही चीज़ हैं? ज़्यादातर लोग यहीं ग़लत सोचते हैं।" / "Are demat and trading accounts the same? Most people get this wrong."
3. "शेयर ख़रीदते ही वो कहाँ जाते हैं?" / "When you buy a share, where does it go?"

## 4. Recommended hook
**Hook 1.** A true statement that creates a gap, then a direct question.

## 11. Chart / data requirements and sources
No price chart; this is a concept video. Facts used:
- A demat account is held with a SEBI-registered depository participant; a trading account is with a SEBI-registered stock broker: https://investor.sebi.gov.in/pdf/reference-material/ppt/PPT-2%20KYC%20Procedure%20%28Opening%20of%20Trading%20and%20Demat%20Account%29.pdf
- T+1: shares bought on a day usually reach the demat account the next working day: https://www.indmoney.com/learn/indian-stocks/t-plus-1-settlement-india and https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/stock-market-settlement-cycles-india-s-shift-from-t-2-to-t-1-explained-123080100560_1.html
- Check the broker's SEBI registration before onboarding: https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/steer-clear-of-unregistered-middlemen-acting-on-behalf-of-brokers-125082500653_1.html and https://www.iepf.gov.in/content/iepf/global/master/Home/InvestorAwareness/resources/dos-and-don-ts/dealing-with-brokers---sub-brokers.html
Simplification: the video says "money is debited from the bank account"; in practice funds usually go through the broker's ledger first. T+1 is "usually", and T+0 exists as an optional mechanism for select stocks.
