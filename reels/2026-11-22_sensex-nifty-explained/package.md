## 1. Topic and unique angle
What Sensex and Nifty 50 actually are. The angle: "Sensex is not a company." It is a scoreboard built from a basket of big companies, and that surprises most beginners.

## 2. Target audience and objective
Hindi-speaking beginners who hear "Sensex fell, Nifty rose" every day without knowing what it means. Objective: a clear mental model (index = scoreboard, not a company, not a stock you buy directly), plus saves and shares.

## 3. Three hook options
1. **"सेंसेक्स कोई कंपनी नहीं है। तो फिर यह है क्या?"** / "Sensex is not a company. So what is it?"
2. "टीवी पर रोज़ सुनते हैं 'सेंसेक्स गिरा'। पर यह है क्या?" / "You hear 'Sensex fell' on TV every day. But what is it?"
3. "सेंसेक्स में 30 शेयर, निफ्टी में 50। फ़र्क़ सिर्फ़ गिनती का है?" / "30 stocks in Sensex, 50 in Nifty. Is the only difference the count?"

## 4. Recommended hook
**Hook 1.** A true, surprising statement followed by a question. It corrects a common assumption in the first two seconds.

## 11. Chart / data requirements and sources
No price chart and no index levels, because this is a concept video; the only figures are definitions and base dates:
- Sensex: 30 constituents, first compiled 1986, base year 1978-79, base value 100, free-float market-cap weighted: https://bseindia.com/markets/derivatives/derireports/FAQs.aspx
- Nifty 50: 50 companies, base date 3 Nov 1995, base value 1000, launched 22 Apr 1996, free-float weighted: https://www.niftyindices.com/indices/equity/broad-based-indices/nifty--50
- Indices cannot be bought directly; index funds and ETFs track them (an ETF needs a demat account, an index fund does not): https://amfiindia.com/investor-corner/knowledge-center/etf.html and https://www.dspim.com/campaigns/learn-lets-index/
The bar picture in scene 3 is a labelled illustration, not real weights.
